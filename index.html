My research asks two connected questions about personalised treatment in MS.

01

Prognostic prediction

Can machine-learning models estimate relapse, disability and PIRA risk for an individual patient across 12- to 48-month horizons?

02

Counterfactual prediction

How do model-implied risks change when the disease-modifying therapy representation changes, without overstating those contrasts as causal effects?

03

Personalised medicine

Bring calibrated prognostic evidence, careful causal reasoning and clinical interpretation together to study treatment pathways.

01 / Research

From missing data to usable evidence

I design the bridge between a clinical question and a model worth trusting.

That means engineering realistic patient journeys, checking whether they preserve clinical structure, and using interpretable prediction to study disability progression and treatment response. The aim is rigorous research evidence—not automated clinical judgment or a treatment recommendation.

Flagship research direction

Build the patient journey that the dataset cannot show us.

My doctoral work develops clinically grounded synthetic longitudinal cohorts for multiple sclerosis. The challenge is not simply generating plausible rows. It is preserving time, dependency, missingness and disease progression well enough for downstream research to remain meaningful.

02

Doctoral research · private analysis

Crystal Ball 2.0

Developing treatment-conditioned prognostic models with the contemporary 2025 MSBase cohort: nine DMT groups, relapse, CDW, CDI and PIRA outcomes, and 12-, 24- and 48-month horizons. The workflow combines patient-disjoint validation, calibrated XGBoost, S- and T-learners, bootstrap uncertainty, decision curves and SHAP. V15 is being prepared as the final reproducible analysis.
