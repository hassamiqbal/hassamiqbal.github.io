01 The research question

Why this project exists

Longitudinal MS data are valuable, scarce and difficult to share.

Multiple sclerosis unfolds over years, but real-world records are irregular: visits occur at different times, treatments change, outcomes are missing and patient privacy restricts access.

The project asks whether a generative framework can preserve useful population and temporal structure while producing new, governed trajectories for research—not digital copies of individuals.

Core question
Can synthetic longitudinal cohorts support credible MS research while reducing dependence on direct access to patient-level records?

04 Validation evidence

More than visual similarity

Fidelity, realism, utility and privacy were examined separately.

No single score establishes that a synthetic cohort is useful or safe. The evaluation therefore combines population comparisons, temporal tests, discrimination, blinded clinical review, downstream utility and privacy-proxy diagnostics.

What the evidence supports

SIMS preserved several important population features and produced trajectories that clinicians could not reliably separate from real records in a small blinded review.

What remains imperfect

Longitudinal EDSS, relapse burden, disease duration, treatment-tier composition and temporal dependence retained detectable differences.

Privacy claim boundary

Membership inference and nearest-neighbour analyses are privacy-proxy diagnostics only. They are not formal re-identification tests or differential-privacy guarantees.
